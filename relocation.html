The First 30 Days — Practical Checklist

  • Arizona Driver's License — Day 1 priority. Arizona law requires you to obtain a state license within 30 days of establishing residency. Your first day of work establishes residency. Your International Driving Permit (IDP) from Taiwan is recognized but has significant limitations — insurance companies may dispute claims if you do not have a state license. Book your MVD appointment at azmvdnow.gov immediately upon arrival. Walk-in wait times regularly exceed 2 hours; with an appointment you are out in 30 minutes. You need: Arizona address proof (lease agreement or utility bill), passport, H-1B visa documents, and SSN or SSN application receipt (MVD can process without SSN if you have the receipt).
  • U.S. Bank Account. Open as soon as possible — your first paycheck and your future mortgage down payment both depend on a U.S. account with a documented history. Major banks (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo) all have North Phoenix branches along the I-17 corridor.
  • SSN Application. If not provided before arrival, visit the nearest Social Security Administration office. SSN typically arrives 2–4 weeks after application. You will need it for most banking, credit applications, and your mortgage pre-qualification.
  • ITIN (if needed). If you need to file taxes or open certain financial accounts before receiving your SSN, an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) from the IRS is the alternative. A Certified Acceptance Agent can assist with ITIN applications — ask Dominion for referrals to qualified agents in North Phoenix.
  • Health Insurance. TSMC Arizona provides health benefits, but the structure differs significantly from Taiwan's National Health Insurance. Read your benefits package carefully. The nearest urgent care facilities to Norterra are Honor Health Sonoran Medical Hospital and several urgent care clinics on the I-17 corridor.
  • School Enrollment. For families with school-age children, enroll immediately. DVUSD and CCUSD both require proof of address, proof of age, and immunization records. Arizona immunization requirements differ from Taiwan — your pediatrician can advise on what boosters may be needed. Confirm which specific school serves your rental address before signing a lease if school district matters to you.

Driving in Arizona — What Surprises Taiwanese Drivers

Phoenix is not walkable. A car is not optional — it is your lifeline to work, groceries, school, and everything else. The sooner you are licensed and comfortable driving, the better your daily life will be.

  • Right on red: Legal in Arizona unless a sign prohibits it. You must come to a complete stop first, then check for pedestrians and traffic. This rule does not exist in Taiwan.
  • Full stop at stop signs: Rolling stops are the number one reason people fail the Arizona road test. Wheels must not be moving for at least 3 seconds. In Taiwan, many drivers slow without fully stopping — Arizona officers ticket this aggressively.
  • Pedestrian right-of-way: Pedestrians in crosswalks have absolute right-of-way. Failure to yield is a moving violation.
  • Photo enforcement: Fixed and mobile speed cameras mail tickets directly to your home. They are common on Phoenix freeways and surface streets.
  • No vehicle inspections: Arizona does not require periodic safety inspections (unlike Taiwan). However, vehicles registered in Maricopa County must pass an emissions test every two years.
  • Speed: Residential areas are typically 25 mph (40 km/h). Freeways are posted 65–75 mph (105–120 km/h). The I-17 and Loop 303 are the primary TSMC commute routes — learn the on-ramps and preferred exits before your first day.

The Taiwanese Community in North Phoenix

When TSMC announced its Arizona campus, North Phoenix had almost no Asian grocery infrastructure. That has changed significantly since 2022. Per reporting in the New York Times and Taiwan News, Taiwanese businesses now dot North Phoenix strip malls along the I-17 and Carefree Highway corridors. Supermarkets have begun stocking Taiwanese food. A developer is converting a mall near the campus area into an Asian shopping center. Boba tea shops, Taiwanese restaurants, and businesses serving the community have opened and continue to open. Mandarin is heard at daycare centers and schools near the fab.

The community pattern from Hsinchu — where TSMC's Taiwan headquarters is located — is partially reproducing in North Phoenix: technical families in proximity, a shared culture, community spaces emerging around food and language. It is not Hsinchu. But it is no longer the culturally isolated situation employees faced when they first arrived in 2022.

Asian Groceries & Taiwanese Food

The Asian grocery infrastructure in North Phoenix has improved significantly since 2022 but is still developing. Current options:

  • 99 Ranch Market — the major Taiwanese-owned Asian supermarket chain — has locations in the Phoenix metro area. The closest to North Phoenix are in the East Valley (Mesa/Chandler) and central Phoenix. A North Phoenix location is anticipated as the TSMC community grows.
  • H Mart — Korean-focused but carries extensive Taiwanese and Pan-Asian products — has locations in Chandler and Scottsdale. 30–40 minute drive from Norterra.
  • Super L Ranch Market — formerly the anchor of Phoenix's Chinese Cultural Center — relocated to South Scottsdale but has since closed. This gap has not yet been fully replaced in the North Phoenix corridor.
  • Local strip mall Asian markets: Smaller Taiwanese and Chinese grocery stores have opened in strip malls along the I-17 and Carefree Highway corridors since 2022. Ask Dominion's office on Carefree Highway for current recommendations — the landscape is changing faster than any published guide can track.
  • Standard Phoenix grocery chains: Fry's, Safeway, Whole Foods, and Sprouts carry increasing Asian product lines in response to the TSMC community's arrival. The Fry's and Safeway stores on the I-17 corridor near Norterra have meaningfully expanded their Asian food sections since 2022.

Phoenix Weather — What to Expect from Taiwan

Taiwanese employees arriving from Hsinchu or Taipei experience Phoenix climate as genuinely extreme — in both directions. Summer highs regularly exceed 110°F (43°C) from June through September. Winter lows occasionally reach near freezing at night from December through February, though snow is essentially unknown in the Phoenix metro. The Norterra and Cave Creek corridor sits at roughly 1,800–2,100 feet elevation — slightly cooler than downtown Phoenix, but the difference is modest.

  • June–September: Monsoon season. Flash floods are real — do not drive through flooded washes. The Phoenix Sonoran Preserve trails close during extreme heat advisories. Mornings before 7am and evenings after 7pm are the viable outdoor windows in summer.
  • October–May: Why Phoenix exists. Outdoor dining, hiking, golf, and farmers markets all peak. This is the reason Carefree has a Friday farmers market year-round and the Cave Creek trail systems fill up with riders and runners.
  • Electricity costs: Summer air conditioning bills are significantly higher than Taiwan or most U.S. markets. Budget $250–$450/month for electricity in a typical 2,000 sq ft home during June–September. Well-insulated new construction (Norterra, Halo Vista) significantly reduces this cost.
