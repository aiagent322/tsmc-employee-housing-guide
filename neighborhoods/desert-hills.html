What Desert Hills Is

Desert Hills is not a master-planned community and it is not a single development. It is a semi-rural residential corridor in the 85086 zip code between Anthem to the south and New River to the north, east of I-17. Homes sit on lots ranging from 1 to 5+ acres with private wells, septic systems, and no master homeowners association. The lack of HOA means no restrictions on RVs, workshops, guest casitas, site modifications, or large vehicles — freedom that is genuinely rare at this proximity to a major employer.

The TSMC Appeal

For TSMC employees — particularly those from Taiwan accustomed to smaller urban footprints who are experiencing their first opportunity to purchase on meaningful land — Desert Hills offers something no master-planned community can: a custom home on acreage with a 20–25 minute commute. I-17 south to Dove Valley Road west gets you to the fab entrance in approximately 20–28 minutes from most Desert Hills addresses in normal morning traffic. This is longer than Norterra but shorter than Cave Creek or Carefree, and the trade-off is privacy, acreage, and no HOA.

School District — Verify Per Parcel

This is the most important due diligence item in Desert Hills. The 85086 zip code spans two school districts: Deer Valley Unified School District (DVUSD, ADE "A" rated) and Cave Creek Unified School District (CCUSD, Niche B+). The same street can have parcels in different districts depending on exact location. Never rely on the zip code or mailing address. Pull the specific parcel address through both the DVUSD school finder and the CCUSD enrollment portal before writing an offer. Dominion does this verification as a standard part of every Desert Hills transaction.

Well & Septic — Required Due Diligence

Every Desert Hills acreage property uses a private well and septic system. Before closing on any Desert Hills property: verify well permit status with the Arizona Department of Water Resources (ADWR) at azwater.gov; conduct a pump test to confirm well yield; obtain water quality testing; and have the septic system inspected by a licensed engineer. Arizona law requires a septic inspection within six months before ownership transfer and a Notice of Transfer filed within 15 days after closing. Dominion has established relationships with the right inspectors and knows which well depths and water quality profiles are typical for specific Desert Hills sub-areas.
